The Problem

You already know average speed. But what about right now?

If a car travels 120 miles in 2 hours, its average speed is 60 mph. Easy. But what was its speed at exactly 1:47 PM? Not over an interval. Not approximately. Exactly — at that single instant in time.

That question has no answer in algebra. It takes calculus to answer it. And calculus answers it by asking: what happens when the interval shrinks to nothing?

The calculus connection: The instantaneous rate of change — the derivative — is what the average rate of change approaches as the time interval collapses to a single point. You will build that idea today from the ground up.

The Algebra You Know IS Calculus

You've been doing this all along.

Today's key discovery: the difference quotient is not new algebra. It's the average rate of change written precisely — and when you let h approach zero, algebra becomes calculus. The derivative was hiding in your algebra the whole time.
